What is flexibility worth?

A factory needs heat around the clock. A normal factory buys electricity as it burns it, so it pays whatever the price happens to be. A thermal battery can buy early and hold the heat — so it can wait for the cheap hours. Drag the prices below and watch what that waiting is actually worth.

What this leaves out

The battery knows the future here. It picks the cheapest hours as if the whole day were known in advance. A real operator is guessing at breakfast what the afternoon will cost. The real project runs it twice — once knowing, once guessing — and the gap between them is what good forecasting is worth.

Real markets have layers. Power is bought a day ahead and trued up in real time, and the network fee here stands in for a stack of charges that differ by country and by customer. This shows the shape of the value, not anyone's actual economics.

The day is 24 hours, not a year. A real answer needs all 8,760 hours of published prices, because the cheap hours cluster differently by season — and that clustering is most of the story.
