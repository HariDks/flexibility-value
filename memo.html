Memorandum

What decides whether a flexible industrial load saves money

Prepared by Hari Dharshini Koundinya Swaminathen September 2026 Spain, South Australia, Minnesota 25 years of published prices

A factory making heat from electricity pays two bills. One for the power. One to the network for delivering it, and that second bill is usually set by the single biggest burst of power the factory ever draws.

A thermal battery wins the first bill by buying in short hard bursts, which is exactly what the second bill punishes. Across three markets and 25 years of published prices, the second bill decided the answer every time. What mattered was which hours the network is watching when it looks for your biggest burst.

What the analysis found

South Australia settles the mechanism. Two networks, one state, same prices, same battery, nine years. The one watching only summer evenings beat the one watching always by 19 to 44 percentage points, in every single year. Connected to ElectraNet the battery loses money outright in 15 of 36 year and site combinations. Nothing differs between them but the billing rule.

Minnesota confirms it, and already contains the fix. On Otter Tail Power's standard tariff a battery loses money in all 36 combinations of pricing point and year tested. Under its Thermal Market Energy Pricing tariff, which bills demand on a figure agreed in advance rather than the metered peak, the same battery wins in all eight years. Nothing changes but one sentence in the rate schedule. TMEP is filed and approved.

The price of power matters far less than the billing rule. Spain's average wholesale price ranged five-fold, €34 to €168 per MWh, while the saving moved 17.1% to 16.8%. What matters is not the level but how far the cheapest hours sit below the daily average.

Against gas, the case has improved fast. A factory chooses against a gas boiler, not an electric heater, so the comparison turns on carbon. Rather than argue about the right carbon price, the analysis solves for the price at which the two are equal. In Spain that has fallen from €101 a tonne in 2018 to below zero in 2025: the battery now wins even if carbon is free. Europe charges €75. The qualification is that 2025 is the best year in the series, so the trend is the finding rather than the single year.

Where this is weakest

Australian visibility. Six hours of useful price sight was assumed. At twenty-four the South Australian saving runs 49.9% to 73.1%, so 23 points rest on that one assumption. Against the forecasts Australia actually published, the battery captured 37% of perfect knowledge at six hours and 80% at twelve.

TMEP's agreed baseline is not in the tariff. Ten megawatts was assumed. At fifteen the saving falls to 7.3%; at twenty it turns negative. The mechanism is the finding, but the negotiated number is where the value is decided.

South Australia is the best of five, not a typical one. Holding the tariff fixed and swapping regions gives 22.6% in Tasmania against 49.9% in South Australia. Read it as the top of a range.

What I would ask you

What it costs a customer when heat stops, which is the missing term in every firmness number here. What baseline demand is realistically achievable in a TMEP-style negotiation. And whether the right benchmark is a gas boiler or an inflexible electric one, because this reports both and they answer different questions.

Model. One factory needing 10 MW of heat continuously, with a thermal battery holding 12 hours which can be filled at four times its average draw, compared against the same factory with no battery buying power as it uses it. Tariffs held at 2025 values so only prices vary.
Excluded. What the battery costs to build, the value of heat when the grid fails, and any income beyond buying power at better times. This is not a payback calculation.
Sources. OMIE, AEMO, MISO market reports and archives. CNMC and the BOE, SA Power Networks, ElectraNet, Otter Tail Power. Eurostat, the AER and the EIA for gas.

Three designs. South Australia pays you to stay out: avoiding 5pm to 9pm is worth 8.9% before a single cheap hour is chased. Spain does not pay you to stay out, but stops you being punished: staying in the cheap bands alone loses 4.9%, and its whole value is that it lets a big burst land where it is charged almost nothing, which is why doing both beats the two parts added together by 9.7 points. Minnesota punishes both moves, and there is no clock to play.
