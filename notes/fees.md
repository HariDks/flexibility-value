# Network fees and price access, by market

**Step 4 deliverable.** This file is the hard part of the project and the reason
it is a policy memo rather than a spreadsheet. Fill it by reading regulator
documents, not by estimating.

For each market, answer two questions:

1. **Can an industrial consumer of this size buy at hourly wholesale prices at
   all?** In much of the US the answer is no without a special tariff — which is
   exactly why Otter Tail had to file TMEP and get three commissions to approve
   it.
2. **What is charged per MWh delivered on top of the wholesale price?** Network
   charges, levies, taxes — everything that applies regardless of *when* the
   power was bought.

Record the number, the source, and an honest confidence rating. **If it cannot be
established from public documents, say so — that is a finding, not a gap.**

## Cross-market observation to test

The working thesis is that **the non-energy share of delivered cost, not
wholesale volatility, decides how much flexibility is worth.** If that holds, the
market with the wildest prices will not necessarily be the market where a thermal
battery saves the most. Check whether the data supports this before asserting it.
