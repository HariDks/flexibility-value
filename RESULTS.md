# Results — the single current statement

Every number here is regenerated by `src/results.py` and the analysis scripts.
Where working notes or earlier conversation disagree, **this file is correct.**

**Scope.** A factory needing 10 MW of heat continuously, a 12-hour store,
charging at 4× average draw, 1%/day standby loss, each market's real forecast
horizon (24h Spain and MISO, 6h South Australia). **25 market-years:** Spain
2018–2025, South Australia 2017–2025, MISO 2018–2025. Tariffs are held at their
2025 values throughout so that only prices vary.

### Correction — this claim was overstated

Earlier framing said the same battery is *"worth +50% on one network and −19% on
another."* **That holds only at a 6× charge rate.** At the 4× base case used
throughout, ElectraNet turns positive in recent years, as the table shows.

**What survives, and it is enough:** SA Power Networks beats the *best*
ElectraNet location **by 19 to 44 points, in every one of nine years.** The
time-aware charge is worth roughly 20–40 percentage points over the time-blind
one, always, with no currency, weather or market-design confound. ElectraNet is
outright negative in 15 of 36 year-locations, and in *all four* locations in
2017 and 2020.

**Use the gap, not the sign flip.**

## 4. The findings

**1. What decides whether flexibility pays is whether the charge knows what time
it is.** Demonstrated within one country across nine years (§3), and across
three markets over 25 market-years (§2).

**2. Three kinds of tariff.** *(2025, `analyse_decomposition.py`)*

| | Price hunting alone | Tariff clock alone | Together | Interaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spain | +33.9% | **−4.9%** | +38.7% | **+9.7** |
| South Australia | +39.6% | **+8.9%** | +49.8% | +1.3 |
| MISO | −59.1% | −2.6% | −10.9% | +50.8 |

**Spain is permissive** — the clock earns nothing alone but stops price-hunting's
peak being punished. **South Australia is rewarding** — the clock pays by itself,
no forecasting needed. **MISO is punitive** — every lever loses.

**3. TMEP flips the incentive, but the value sits in a negotiated number.**
Demand is billed on an agreed *Baseline Demand*, set in the service agreement
and not specified by the tariff:

| Agreed baseline | vs inflexible |
|---|---|
| **10 MW** (firm load — assumed) | **+20.6%** |
| 15 MW | +7.3% |
| 20 MW | **−6.0%** |
| 40 MW (actual peak — no deal) | −59.3% |

**The headline is a fact about the tariff plus an assumption about a
negotiation.** The mechanism is right; the baseline is where value is decided.

**4. Flexibility value tracks the depth of the cheap hours, not the price
level and not the raw spread.** Spain's mean ran €34 (2020) to €168 (2022) —
five-fold — while the saving moved 17.1% to 16.8%. South Australia ran A$43 to
A$156 with 39.0% and 45.6%. So price *level* is irrelevant.

Tested across the five NEM regions at one moment, raw peak-to-trough spread
ranks them **wrong**: New South Wales has the largest mean daily spread of any
region (A$431) and only the third-best saving. Rank correlation with the
saving: price level −0.30, spread +0.50, cheap-hours gap +0.70, **that gap as a
share of the mean price +0.90**.

**The screening rule: how far the cheap hours sit below the day's average, as a
fraction of that average.** Max minus min is set by spikes, and a battery never
buys spikes. See `notes/nodes.md`.

**5. Arbitrage storage stops at the forecast horizon — it does not taper.**
Spain flat from 24h, South Australia from ~6h, MISO from 24h. **The limit is
informational, not economic or physical.**

**6. Two different sizing questions.** Median gap without cheap power is 8–13
hours (arbitrage); the 99th percentile is 99–164 hours (firmness). This study
prices only the first. Antora publicly describes multi-day storage for always-on
supply — a different problem needing a different answer.

**7. Visibility is free in Spain and MISO, decisive in South Australia.** Both
publish day-ahead prices — more warning than a 12-hour store needs. The NEM
publishes forecasts but no committable price at any horizon.

## 7. What this does not cover

- **Firmness.** Storage priced only as an arbitrage asset. Reliability value,
  curtailment ride-through and multi-day firming are not modelled.
- **Capital cost.** Operating cost only. Connection capex scales with charge
  rate and would push against high charge rates.
- **Real-time markets.** Day-ahead prices where a day-ahead market exists.
- **Temperature.** Heat treated as heat; no process-temperature constraint.
- **TMEP's coincidence condition** — 1.3–2.4% at a realistic farm size,
  excluded from the headline.
- **The forward risk premium** on fixed-price contracts — could not be sourced,
  so not claimed.
- **Price node and scale.** MINN.HUB and SA1 chosen for relevance; one plant
  size throughout.

## 8. Corrections log

1. *"Spain's tariff periods are misaligned with solar."* Wrong — P1 exists only
   Jan/Feb/Jul/Dec; April–May contain none.
2. *"South Australia's demand charge is the harshest."* Wrong and backwards.
3. *Spanish system charges €8–20/MWh.* Wrong by 5–10×; published €0.21–2.58.
4. *MISO factory pays $36.32.* Baseline excluded the Energy Adjustment and
   riders; correct figure $62.47.
5. *"The battery knows the future."* Overstated — foresight was always bounded
   by what the tank carries.
6. *Greedy within 0.2–0.7% of optimal.* Measured on an easier problem; 1.5%
   with tariff caps.
7. *SA peak window is year-round.* Wrong — November to March, on a daily average.
8. *"Returns diminish past 12–24 hours."* They stop, at the forecast horizon.
9. *"90 hours idle proves standby losses are low."* Withdrawn — that is
   discharge duration, not heat retention.
10. *"In Minnesota the battery loses."* A 2025 statement; five of eight years
    reverse it at low charge rates. The **gradient** is what holds.
11. *"MISO data stops at 2023."* Wrong twice — the monthly archives reach 2018.
12. *"Same battery +50% on one network, −19% on another."* True only at 6×
    charge rate. **Use the 19–44 point gap instead.**
13. *"Flexibility value tracks intraday spread."* Right that level does not
    matter, wrong about the measure. Raw spread misranks the five NEM regions;
    **the depth of the cheap hours relative to the daily mean is the predictor**
    (+0.90 against +0.50).
14. *"South Australia's saving is a market result."* Partly a South Australian
    one. Holding the SAPN tariff fixed and swapping regions gives 22.6%
    (Tasmania) to 49.9% (South Australia). The mechanism holds everywhere;
    **SA is the top of a range, not a typical case.**
